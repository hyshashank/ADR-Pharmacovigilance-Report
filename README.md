## Topics Covered

### 1. Classification of ADRs

- **Pharmacological:** Predictable, generally dose-dependent effects
- **Pathological:** Toxic effects or tissue/organ injury
- **Genotoxic:** Damage to genetic material
- **Allergic/Hypersensitivity:** Immune-mediated reactions, Types I–IV
- **Idiosyncratic:** Unpredictable individual reactions
- **Pharmacogenetic:** Responses influenced by genetic variation
- **Teratogenic:** Drug-related fetal harm

### 2. Examples

| ADR Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Pharmacological | Hypotension with calcium-channel blockers |
| Pathological | Liver damage after paracetamol overdose |
| Genotoxic | Effects associated with some anticancer drugs |
| Pharmacogenetic | Primaquine-induced hemolysis in G6PD deficiency |
| Teratogenic | Fetal harm associated with certain medicines |

### 3. Traditional Pregnancy-Risk Categories Discussed in the Video

| Category | Summary | Example |

### 4. Patient Case Analysis

For a patient case, the analysis should follow:

1. Identify the symptoms and clinical findings.
2. Review all current and recently used medicines.
3. Establish the timeline between drug exposure and reaction.
4. Consider alternative causes.
5. Assess dechallenge when clinically appropriate.
6. Identify the most plausible suspected drug.
7. Apply a structured causality assessment.

### 5. Causality Assessment

The video discusses:

- **WHO-UMC Scale**
- **Naranjo Algorithm**
- **Bradford Hill Criteria**

These methods help assess the strength of the relationship between a suspected medicine and an adverse reaction.

### 6. Pharmacovigilance

Pharmacovigilance involves the detection, assessment, understanding and prevention of adverse effects and other medicine-related problems. It is an important component of patient safety and rational medicine use.

### 7. ADR Reporting in India

The video explains reporting suspected ADRs through:

- **ADR PvPI mobile application**
- **Official suspected ADR reporting forms**
- **ADR Monitoring Centres (AMCs)**
- Appropriate **PvPI/CDSCO reporting channels**

Reports should contain relevant patient information while maintaining confidentiality, details of the suspected medicine, other relevant medicines, reaction details, actions taken and seriousness of the event.
